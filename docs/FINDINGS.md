# Verified facts and open questions

First written 2026-10-10, extended 2026-10-11. Everything here was checked read-only or with a few dollars of real funds on Monad mainnet; nothing is taken from memory.

**Method.** Kuru SDK types and ABIs were read from the published npm package. Chain state was read from the public Monad RPC, including simulated calls with state overrides on a throwaway address (no real funds involved). The first reads were taken around block 112,187,000.

## Tool self-tests (read-only, no real funds)

- 17 unit tests passed at that point: unit conversion, the price ladder, and the reconciliation, including the checks for a reversed `Trade.isBuy` and for gas charged on limit versus on used gas.
- `preflight` ran against mainnet: market recognised, tokens and decimals right, SDK parameters equal to the contract's, a valid order accepted, and both controls (below minimum, crossing the book) rejected as expected.
- `plant --sim-funds` (one and three orders), the refusal paths of `deposit` and `plant` for an unfunded address, and `status`, `unwind` (dry run) and `report` (fake state plus a real on-chain order) all ran without program errors.
- SDK transaction construction: `MarginDeposit`, `MarginWithdraw` and `OrderCanceler` use a legacy gas price (`eth_gasPrice`) and a gas limit of 1.2 times the estimate; `PositionProvider.provisionLiquidity` uses the ethers defaults (EIP-1559; Monad's base fee is 100 gwei, ethers suggests a max fee of 201.5 gwei and a 1.5 gwei tip). The SDK's own `eth_estimateGas` call (gas price 1 gwei) on a fake balance returns the contract's error (`InsufficientBalance`), not a gas-price error, so that path works on Monad.

## Real-funds test (2026-10-10)

- **Deposit of 6 USDC.** Approve exactly 6 USDC (the allowance is fully used and returns to zero), then deposit into Kuru's margin account. On chain: margin USDC 6.000000, wallet USDC 7.963306 to 1.963306. The tool's check said the margin grew by exactly the deposited amount, and an independent read-only query agreed.
- **Monad charges the gas limit.** In both receipts `gasUsed` equals `gasLimit` (approve 87,643; deposit 197,626), and the wallet's MON decreased by 0.0290536165, exactly the sum of `gasLimit x effectiveGasPrice`. So a receipt's `gasUsed` is the limit and cannot reveal actual execution use. The SDK sets its limit to 1.2 times the estimate (about 20% more on the deposit, roughly 0.003 MON, negligible). The two fees together were 0.0290536 MON (about $0.0007).
- **Order and fill (order id 122927284).** One flip bid, 201 MON at 0.024761 (one tick below the best ask), flip price 0.024786; gas 281,890 (limit equals use), about 0.0286 MON.
  - Two blocks (about 0.6 s) after creation it was filled completely: `Trade` at 0.024761 for 201 MON, remaining 0. The taker was a contract (`0x34Ae…3fC5`), the `txOrigin` a person (`0xc88d…2E5A`).
  - **In the same block the opposite order was created under a new id** (122927286, not the original): sell 201 MON at 0.024786, whose own flip price is the original bid price 0.024761, so after the sell fills the loop returns to a buy. Its size equals the filled size (maker fee 0, nothing deducted). `s_orders` still shows `flippedId` as 0.
  - **`Trade.isBuy` is the taker's direction, not the maker order's:** when our bid is hit by a seller, the event says `isBuy=false`. The tool first read it the other way; the reconciliation's "sign flipped" diagnostic caught it immediately, it was fixed and a test was added.
  - **Reconciliation passed:** balance changes equal what the fills explain: USDC difference 0, MON difference 0 (gas by limit).
  - **A first sample of adverse selection:** the bid sat at the highest allowed price and was hit at once; the market then moved down (best ask 0.024725), leaving a paper loss of about one cent. Aggressive orders are more likely to be hit by informed takers, which is why the ledger must show such losses honestly.
- **Cancel.** For the order produced by the flip (122927286), `PositionWithdrawer.withdrawLiquidity` (on-chain `batchCancelFlipOrders`) succeeded directly, with no fall-back to `WrongOrderTypeCancel`; event `FlipOrdersCanceled([122927286], owner)`; gas 178,169.
- **Withdraw.** `batchWithdrawMaxTokens([USDC, native MON])` succeeded; margin USDC and MON both returned to zero; gas 190,054.
- **Final reconciliation (after all five transactions).** USDC balance change -4.976961 equals the change implied by the fills (-4.976961), difference 0. MON balance change +200.90486488; adding back gas of 0.09513511 gives +201, difference 0.
- **Cost of the whole test.** Gas for the five transactions totalled 0.095135 MON (about $0.0023); with the small paper loss after the fill the test cost roughly one to two cents. The wallet ended with 2.986345 USDC and 239.957 MON (about 201 MON of it bought).
- **Result: passed.** Creation, a complete fill, the new-id opposite order, cancel, withdraw and reconciliation (difference 0) were all read from chain data. Not observed in this test: partial fills, the full cycle in which the sell fills and flips back into a buy, and when `FlipOrderUpdated` appears (see stage C below).

## Stage C: ledger core and new findings (2026-10-11, read-only)

**Method.** Read-only. The public RPC was scanned by event topic over about two million recent blocks (about seven days, blocks 110,224,556 to 112,224,556, 90 blocks per request, any contract address) to find every Kuru flip-order event, then the receipts of those transactions were read. No transaction was sent.

### Newly verified

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| In seven days only two addresses on the whole chain used flip orders: our test wallet and one other address, `0x8a8b…8ca5`, on a different market (`0x52ce…74ce`; base asset `0x834d…512f` with 6 decimals; quote USDC; price precision 1e6, size precision 1e10, tick 10; maker and taker fees both 0) | scan result of 129 events; `getMarketParams()` |
| **Partial fill:** when an order is only partly filled, `FlippedOrderCreated` appears in the same transaction and **its `flippedId` equals the parent order id** (for a complete fill the field is 0) | five partial fills (blocks 110,298,080 to 111,050,713) |
| **Meaning of `FlipOrderUpdated(child, size)`:** when the parent fills again (partly or the remainder), no new order is created; the earlier child order's size is set to its **new total**; the event sits right before the matching `Trade` | four consistent before-and-after samples, for example child 6632 from 2,240,023,084,024 to 5,010,778,347,784, where the increase equals the fill converted by the rule below, digit for digit |
| **Flip size rule:** when a sell fills, the new buy has size floor(filled size x sell price / flip price), so all USDC proceeds are put back into the buy and the position grows with every round trip; when a buy fills, the new sell has the filled size | all 121 sell fills match digit for digit (including the five partial fills); one buy fill (our test) |
| `Trade.updatedSize` is the maker order's remaining size after this fill | every fill satisfies previous remaining minus filled size equals `updatedSize` |
| `batchWithdrawMaxTokens` emits one `Withdrawal` event per token | raw logs of our withdraw transaction |

### The ledger core (code complete)

- `src/` (TypeScript, runs directly on Node, no new dependencies): event decoding, unit conversion with explicit rounding (amounts received round down, amounts paid or locked round up), orders and flip chains, balances (free, locked, total), fills, fees, gas (by limit), realized profit and loss (per round trip), unrealized profit and loss (at the mid price and at the immediately executable price), position growth, inventory imbalance, reconciliation and a resumable incremental scanner. The design is in `docs/LEDGER.md`.
- Real-data verification: at the six balance snapshots of the real-funds test the ledger's free and locked amounts equal the chain digit for digit; the change in wallet plus margin equals fills minus fees minus gas, difference 0. A live read-only rescan of the same block range (stopped and resumed halfway), reconciled against chain state at the two historical blocks, also gave difference 0 on every check.
- Randomized differential tests: an independently written reference simulator runs random place, partial fill, full fill, flip, cancel, deposit and withdraw sequences over more than 400 random markets (different precisions, native or ERC-20 base asset, fees from 0 to 250 bps, each rounding randomly up or down). Every step is reconciled and every difference stays within the rounding tolerance; with no rounding the difference is exactly 0.
- Negative test: reading `Trade.isBuy` as the maker's direction is detected (a direction contradiction, a failed reconciliation and a "direction read backwards" hint).
- 51 tests in total, all passing.

### Still unverified

1. How a non-zero fee is deducted (both markets seen so far charge 0). The code assumes "taken from the asset received, rounded up" and says so in its messages; flipped sizes always follow the events.
2. How the contract rounds amounts that do not divide evenly. A rounding tolerance covers it (at most one smallest unit per inexact conversion).
3. A `FlipOrderUpdated` that appears after the child was cancelled or filled, or with no later fill of the parent. Never seen; the ledger marks that order's remaining size as unknown, which only skips the free and locked split; totals and profit and loss are unaffected.
4. A fill that produces no new flipped order (for example a size below the minimum). Never seen; the ledger reports "cannot explain", counts the proceeds in the total and treats them as free for now.
5. How many blocks Monad needs before a block cannot be rolled back. The scanner stays two blocks behind by default; that is a choice, not a verified result.
6. An `approve` transaction emits no Kuru event, so the scanner cannot find its gas; the caller has to add it (this only affects the wallet-total check).
7. The `gasUsed` of 8,100,000 reported by `debug_traceCall` in simulation is probably just the call limit, not real usage; real gas comes from receipts.
8. The address and minimum order of a WETH-USDC fallback market (Kuru's docs list only MON-AUSD and MON-USDC; AUSD saw no trades in 24 hours).

### Implications for the product

- Each price level costs at least about $5, so the number of levels is roughly the budget divided by $5: a $50 budget gives at most about ten levels.
- "Position growth" now has an on-chain basis: on every round trip the proceeds of the sell go back into a larger buy, so the ledger can show, per flip chain, how much each round earned and how much the position grew.
- Flip orders are barely used on mainnet (one external user in seven days). That is a gap, and it also means few samples: the rules above rest on the real data of those two accounts.

## Stage D, milestone 1: the read-only ledger page (2026-10-11)

- **What was built**: `web/` (plain TypeScript; the only new dependency is esbuild, a development dependency used only inside `web/`; the page imports `../src` directly and copies no core code). The data layer `web/src/pipeline.ts` reads at most 90 blocks per `eth_getLogs` call, can be cancelled and resumed, and caches results in the browser's localStorage per address and start block. The presentation layer `web/src/model.ts` is made of pure functions (all amounts are integers plus fixed-point formatting, no floating point) and is asserted against the real-funds data by `test/web-model.test.ts`; the page itself is `main.ts` and `style.css`. 59 tests in total (the original 51 are untouched).
- **Mainnet check** (the same code run read-only in Node on the example wallet, blocks 112,209,462–112,211,600, 24 windows, about 15 seconds the first time and about 4 seconds with the cache): all 6 reconciliation checks have difference 0; USDC net change −4.976961; MON net change +201; gas 0.095135 MON; one bid filled in full, one new-id sell order (#122927286) created by the flip, then cancelled. Opening the local page in Chrome and pressing "Try the example wallet" shows exactly these numbers (desktop browser).
- **The gas of `approve` is now found**: `approve` emits no Kuru event, but USDC's `Approval(owner, spender)` event has indexed owner and spender fields, so it can be filtered by wallet. The data layer sends one extra filtered request per window and adds those transactions to the gas total, so the total matches the real test (0.095135 MON, of which `approve` is 0.0088958). The 100-block range limit applies to the narrow filter too (a 1,000-block request was rejected in testing). Item 6 of "Still unverified" above is therefore handled in the page; the core `src/` is unchanged.
- **Basis used on the page**: market value and profit and loss use the order book (best bid and best ask) at the end block, and wallet balances are also read at the end block. If the wallet already held resting orders or balances before the start block, reconciliation will not match (the page suggests an earlier start).
- **Phone width, checked on the live page** (2026-10-11): the browser's window-resize call reported success but did not change the viewport, so the page was loaded inside a 390 px wide iframe of the same origin, which makes its media queries behave as on a phone. Result: no horizontal overflow, text at least 16 px, the orders and fills tables turn into stacked label-and-value cards, buttons are large. Weak spots: inline links (addresses, transaction hashes, the footer link) are only about 19 to 24 px tall, and the counterparty row in a fill wraps onto three lines. Touch behaviour and a real mobile browser were not tried.
- **Still unverified**: the page on a real phone, and non-zero fees (see above). Wallets outside the example range (partial fills, multi-round flips) have not been tried on the page.
- **Performance**: windows are read one after another (the core's `sync` runs window by window), so long histories are slow (about 0.6 seconds per 90 blocks). Long histories need an indexing service (for example Envio), which is a separate decision.

## Stage D, milestone 2: the planting flow (2026-10-11, built without sending any transaction)

- **What was built**: `src/abi.ts` (dependency-free ABI encoders and decoders for `approve`, the margin account's `deposit`, `batchProvisionLiquidity`, `batchCancelFlipOrders`, `batchWithdrawMaxTokens`, plus revert-reason decoding), `src/plant.ts` (hard caps equal to the config, the bid ladder ported from `lib/units.cjs`, preview maths, the transaction guard, next-step planners), `src/flow.ts` (wallet access, chain reads, the one-transaction engine, post-condition checks, preview simulation), and the page `web/src/plant.ts` at `#plant`. 93 tests in total (the earlier 59 are untouched); no model or change of the ledger core.
- **Test method**: no AI session signed or sent anything. Encoders are compared with `ethers` and the SDK ABI (selectors, 200 random inputs per function, 300 random provision batches); the ladder equals `lib/units.cjs` on 3,000 random books; the engine runs against a fake wallet that only records requests and a fake chain. Covered: user rejection, wrong chain and a wallet that does not know Monad, switched account, failed simulation, a book that moved before the simulation and while the user read the statement, a reload half-way, a failed and a pending transaction, and mismatches that must be reported as "cannot explain". The page was also walked through in Chrome on `web/fake.html` at desktop and 390 px (no horizontal overflow); the ledger page still reproduces the example wallet (6 checks, difference 0). The user's real Chrome also showed that EIP-6963 discovery finds several installed wallets.
- **Design decisions**: sizes are whole coins, so every locked amount is an exact integer and the margin check can demand exact equality; bids are sent with `provisionOrRevert = true` (all or nothing, the simulation shows which); post-conditions are read at the receipt's block; "Unwind" refuses to say "nothing to unwind" when the order lookup failed; planting is offered only when the wallet has no live orders.
- **Found while testing**: the shared JSON-RPC adapter retries every error six times, which would make a reverting simulation wait about 20 seconds; the flow uses a second adapter instance with no retries for simulations. A first version of unwind treated a failed order lookup as "no orders" and reported success; fixed and now stops with an explanation.
- **Checked read-only against the real Monad RPC** (block about 112,362,000, no transaction, a throwaway sender with a state override): the preview simulation accepts a one-level and a three-level ladder (so three orders in one batch simulate fine), and the revert decoder names the real errors: a bid above the ask gives `ProvisionError`, an order of 199 MON gives `SizeError`, with no retry delay.
- **Not verified**: everything involving a real wallet and real money (see `docs/PLANT-TEST-RUNBOOK.md`); `eth_estimateGas` from a real wallet's own address; the page on a real phone; wallet-specific behaviour (the pop-up's wording of an exact approval, chain switching on each wallet).
- **Still unseen on chain**: the full cycle where a flipped sell fills and flips back into a buy, non-zero fees, and several orders in one batch (our own run placed one).

## Stage D, milestone 3, phase 1: Mera login, AUSD swap through Kuru Flow, PWA shell (2026-10-11, built without a real passkey, key or transaction)

- **Dependencies** (the four the owner approved, in `web/package.json` only, exact versions, lockfile committed): `@category-labs/mera` 0.2.0 (published 2026-08-12; the only other version is 0.1.0 of 2026-07-23; the package says it is in preview and may change before 1.0, and that Category Labs completed an internal security review), `viem` 2.56.9 (2026-09-24; newer 2.57.x exist, published 2026-09-29 to 2026-10-08, and were skipped to stay more than two weeks old), `@scure/bip32` 2.4.0 and `@scure/bip39` 2.4.0 (both 2026-08-28). Mera's own runtime dependencies are `@noble/curves`, `@noble/hashes` and `@scure/base`; `viem` brings its own copies of the scure packages (older versions) as transitive dependencies. `npm audit` reported 0 vulnerabilities at install time. No changelog files ship in the packages; versions were chosen by publish date and the package metadata.
- **Key derivation** follows Mera's "Send a transaction with viem" recipe (read 2026-10-11): the passkey's 32 PRF bytes are the BIP-39 entropy, the seed goes through BIP-32 at `m/44'/60'/0'/0/0`, and `createSecp256k1SigningSession` + `toViemAccount` sign. A test derives the same address independently with `ethers` and pins it for fixed stub bytes. Signed transactions (legacy type, chain 143, the node's `eth_gasPrice`) equal what `ethers` signs for the same key and fields in 25 random cases.
- **What stays in memory and what cannot be wiped.** The page zeroes its copies of the PRF bytes, the seed and the derived key right after the session takes its own copy (tested for the PRF bytes). The BIP-39 phrase that the recipe builds is a JavaScript string and cannot be wiped; it waits for garbage collection. The session's own copy is zeroed by `session.end()`, which runs on Lock, after 5 minutes idle, on `pagehide` and (by the page being gone) on reload; a test shows the underlying viem account throws after each trigger. Nothing sensitive reaches `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, the console or any request body (tested with spies; the only requests are JSON-RPC to one URL, and the only state-changing one carries a signed transaction). Browsers that keep a page in the back/forward cache after `pagehide` are a possible gap: the page would be frozen with a session still open until the idle timer fires on resume; not tested on a real browser.
- **Hosts the bundle can contact** (from the minified bundle and a runtime check in Chrome): `https://rpc.monad.xyz` (JSON-RPC reads and the signed transaction), `https://ws.kuru.io` (Kuru Flow: token and quote), and, only when the visitor clicks a link, `monadscan.com` and `github.com`. A `viem.sh` string appears in the bundle as text inside an error message, not as a request. No WebSocket code is present. The service worker never touches another origin.

The brief's rule "check against the config's router" therefore could not be applied literally; the guard pins the Flow router as its own constant and a test keeps the two addresses apart.
  - Calldata: selector `0xce1e7030`; word 0 tokenOut (AUSD), word 1 minOut, word 2 tokenIn (USDC), word 3 amountIn, word 4 a fixed contract `0x62ee…266a` (it has code and did not change with the user address), then zero words, a flag and an opaque route blob. **The user's address is not in the calldata at all.**
  - Simulation with a state override on the user's USDC balance and allowance (USDC uses Circle's storage layout: balance slot 9, allowance slot 10) and `debug_traceCall`: the router pulls exactly 5 USDC from the user with `transferFrom` (this needs an allowance to `0xb3e6…13cb`, so the approval spender is the Flow router), an executor contract routes through Kuru's market and other pools, and **the AUSD arrives back at the router and is then transferred to the caller** (4.992540 AUSD in one trace for 5 USDC). The page therefore also runs `debug_traceCall` from the user's own address right before the pop-up-equivalent button and refuses unless the only token leaving the user is exactly the USDC amount (to the router) and at least `minOut` AUSD goes to the user; after the receipt it requires USDC down by exactly the amount and AUSD up by at least `minOut`. If the public RPC stops offering `debug_traceCall`, swaps are refused rather than sent unchecked.

## Stage D, milestone 3, phase 2: the Perpl hedge (2026-10-11)

**Built, not yet verified with real money.** Details and block numbers: `docs/PERPL-ONCHAIN.md`, "Phase 2 findings". The owner's first real run is `docs/HEDGE-TEST-RUNBOOK.md`.

### Facts settled read-only

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| A funding event happens every **8,571 blocks, about 43 minutes** (0.3019 s per block), not hourly | `getFundingInterval()`; five `FundingEventCompleted` events 8,571 blocks apart; block timestamps |
| Funding per event is **rate / 100,000 of notional** (rate -4 = 0.004%); negative rate: shorts pay longs; `fundingCNS` and premium PnL are credit-positive | payment = price x rate / 1000 in 1/100 price units in all five events; real closes with +3,978 (long, 3,939 lots) and -82 (short, 81 lots) |
| Cost of a short, measured: 0.014% of notional over the last day, 0.157% over 7 days, 0.256% over 14 days; at today's rate -4 it would be 0.134% a day | cumulative `getFundingSumAtBlock` at head and head minus 1, 3, 7 and 14 days (blocks per day 286,197) |
| The extra 0.5% to 0.85% collateral is the **negative PnL of the fill against the mark price**: deposit = notional / leverage + max(0, (mark - fill) x size), capped at 10% of notional | simulated opens at three pinned blocks match to the unit, short and long |
| Fees are millionths of notional: tier 1 taker 3.45 bps, maker 0.45 bps, new accounts start in tier 1, the fee is charged on top of the collateral | `getPerpFeeSchedule`, `getTakerFee`, simulated fills (871 on 2,523,300; 4,878 on 14,138,880) |
| Round trip of a hedge at today's book: about 0.24% of notional (spread plus two taker fees); funding is smaller than that for days | arithmetic on the live book |
| An immediate-or-cancel order priced beyond the book succeeds and fills nothing | simulation: only `OrderRequestV2` and `OrderBatchCompleted` |
| The public RPC answers a reverting view (no account) with a revert; the adapter retried it six times with back-off, which would have made the page wait about 19 s | fixed: reverts are not retried (`src/rpc.ts`), with a test |

### What was built

- **Guard** (`src/perpl/guard.ts`, routed from `guardTx`): five kinds, each decoded from its own calldata: AUSD `approve` to the exchange (exact, at most 15 AUSD), `createAccount` (at most 15 AUSD, at least the live minimum the plan carries), `execOrders` with exactly one OpenShort (market 10, immediate-or-cancel, at most 2x, notional at the price bound at most 15 USD, a deadline block set, `revertOnFail` true) or one CloseShort (reduce-only, same checks), and `withdrawCollateral`. Everything else is refused (longs, resting or post-only orders, cancel, change, deposits, order forwarding, other markets and targets, value). 11 test groups, including calldata built with ethers.
- **Flow** (`src/perpl/hedgeflow.ts`): live context, the hedge plan (whole MON, notional cap, margin cap, deposit, liquidation estimate, funding per day, round-trip estimate), the next action derived from chain state only (reload-safe), a **trace check before every pop-up** (the simulation alone cannot tell that an immediate-or-cancel order would fill nothing), and post-conditions read back from the chain: account created with exactly the deposit and the wallet AUSD down by the same amount; position side, size, collateral and fee against the receipt and the formula; balances; a partial or empty fill is reported as such, not as a failure.
- **Page** (`#hedge`, `web/src/hedge.ts`): inventory (wallet MON above the 0.5 MON reserve plus MON free at Kuru), ratio (default 50%), preview with every number labelled, risks in plain words, a Close hedge button that is shown whenever a position exists, withdraw, partial-fill messages. It works with a browser wallet or the passkey session (both are EIP-1193). A test page with a fake wallet and a fake Perpl chain: `web/fake-hedge.html` (`npm run build:hedge-fake` in `web/`). Checked in Chrome at desktop and 390 px width (no horizontal overflow): open, close and withdraw walked through.
- 33 new tests (guard 11, flow 22, in `test/perpl-guard.test.ts` and `test/perpl-hedgeflow.test.ts`); 177 in total, all passing.

## Stage D, milestone 5: the garden game layer and fixes (2026-10-11, built and tested against the fake wallet and chain only)

- **What was built.** `src/garden.ts` (pure: plots, growth stages, achievements, level; `test/garden.test.ts`), the web layer in `web/src/garden-*.ts` (view model, SVG art, panel, actions, live refresh; `test/garden-web.test.ts`), a read-only copy of the panel on the ledger page, and fake-chain helpers (`fillOrder` emits `Trade` plus the flipped order, `ageBy`, block timestamps). 165 tests pass (the 144 earlier ones are untouched). No new runtime dependency, nothing in the guard, the engine's step order, `web/src/model.ts`, `web/src/pipeline.ts` or the ledger changed.
- **Stage mapping.** seed = planted and nothing filled; sprout = some order of the plot partly filled; flower = exactly one leg completely filled; fruit = two or more legs completely filled (a leg and its flipped opposite leg); pruned = the newest order was cancelled and no round trip was completed. Real data: our own run is one pruned plot (bid filled, flipped sell cut); the other account's 119 plots (market `0x52ce…74ce`) are 117 pruned, 1 flower and 1 sprout.
- **No completed round trip exists in any recorded data.** In that other account every plot is a sell that filled and flipped into a bid, and the bid was cancelled (117 times) or still rests; none has two filled legs. So `ROUND_TRIP_SEEN_ON_REAL_DATA` is false and a test keeps it honest. "Round trips" in the level counts pairs of completely filled legs per plot (three filled legs count as one); the ledger's own cycle count in the Money panel is a different, finer number (it also counts partial matches) and can be higher.
- **Garden age** comes from the timestamps of the first planted order's block and the last block read, never from an assumed block time (public figures for Monad's block time and our own "about seven days for two million blocks" note disagree, so none is used). If the node cannot give the timestamps the age is shown as unknown and the seven-day achievement stays locked.
- **Level thresholds are game design, not measurements:** Sprout = one completed round trip or one day; Bloom = three round trips and three days; Orchard = ten round trips and seven days.
- **Fix (a), stale preview:** pressing "Plant these orders" now reads the book again, rebuilds the preview from the form, tells the visitor if it changed and lists the exact orders in the confirmation statement. Walked through on `web/fake.html` (book nudged up, then Plant: preview prices and the approval amount changed). No automated test, because the page code needs a DOM.
- **Fix (b), "Cannot convert undefined to a BigInt": cause not proven.** It could not be reproduced: not on the fake chain after planting, not against the live RPC in Node from the planting block with a browser-style saved cache, with 0 or 2 confirmations. The most plausible cause found by reading the code is that `JsonRpc.call` returned `undefined` when the node answered with neither `result` nor `error` (a proxy or rate limiter), and several callers pass that straight to `BigInt()`. That is now a failure that is retried and then named (`eth_blockNumber: the node answered without a result`), with a regression test. Two smaller hardenings: the planting note saved in the browser is validated before use (a corrupt or foreign note is ignored), and the order lookup message now says which stage failed. If the error comes back, its text will now name the call.
- **Fix (c), notices:** the intro and the risk list say the flow was verified once on 2026-10-11 with MetaMask and one level, and keep a phone, other wallets, several levels in one batch and a full round trip as unverified.
- **Not verified with real money:** Prune, Harvest, Move and the live refresh have only run against the fake. The first real use should be a small one on a throwaway wallet. A Move whose bid was partly filled below the 200 MON minimum shows "cannot be placed" and does nothing (seen on the fake page).
- **Behaviour to know:** the live refresh pauses while the tab is hidden, so a background tab shows old data until it is visible again (seen while testing in an automated tab). Harvest is account-wide (free money is not tracked per plot) and says so in its statement.

## Leaderboard service (2026-10-11, built and tested against fake KV and a fake chain only; not deployed)

- **What was built.** `server/` (a Cloudflare Worker, no runtime dependency): `POST /register`, `GET /leaderboard`, `GET /garden/:address`, and a Cron Trigger that resumes a bounded scan per wallet and recomputes the figures with the ledger core imported from `../src`. 21 new tests (`test/leaderboard-pure.test.ts`, `test/leaderboard-service.test.ts`). Deploy steps for the owner: `docs/LEADERBOARD-DEPLOY.md`.
- **Ranking** is completed round trips, then garden age in days, then address; never profit. Every API answer carries `verifiedOnRealData`, read from `ROUND_TRIP_SEEN_ON_REAL_DATA` (still `false`: no completed round trip exists in any recorded data). On the two real fixtures the figures are 0 round trips (the gate-test wallet and the other account's 119 plots).
- **Bug found by a test and fixed:** a chain failure in the middle of a scan lost the windows already read, because the state was saved only after the whole scan. It is now saved on failure too, so the cursor never goes backwards.

- **Known limits:** KV has no transactions, so the 500-wallet cap can be exceeded by a few on simultaneous registrations (the cron never ranks more than 500).
